Smart Launcher vs Thin Launcher: Automatic Sorting or a Hard Limit?

Smart Launcher's answer to clutter is to automatically organize everything you install. Thin Launcher's answer is to limit how much you see in the first place. Both reduce chaos — differently.

Simple App Works Published November 13, 2026 7 min read
QUICK ANSWER

Smart Launcher's signature feature is automatic categorization: every app you install gets sorted into a category for you, and the launcher stays actively developed (a version 6.6 update landed in January 2026), with a Pro upgrade historically priced as a one-time purchase, though it's worth checking its current listing since prices and promotions change often. Thin Launcher takes a different, blunter approach — a hard cap of 7 apps free (11 on Pro), full stop, with no categorization system of any kind. One organizes everything; the other restricts how much there is to organize.

Smart Launcher isn't trying to be minimalist in the way Thin Launcher is — it's trying to make a full app list manageable rather than cutting it down. That's a genuinely different strategy for the same underlying complaint ("my home screen is a mess"), and it's worth understanding which strategy actually matches your problem before picking either one.

Neither app is positioned as the "correct" fix here, and this isn't a claim that one beats the other. It's a breakdown of what each one is actually doing under the hood, so you can match the mechanism to the specific thing that bothers you about your current home screen.

How Smart Launcher's categorization actually works

Per its own documentation and changelog, Smart Launcher automatically sorts every app you install into a category — things like Communication, Games, or Tools — rather than asking you to pick a small set of favorites. Version 6.6, released in January 2026, added a new dock system supporting multiple rows, columns, widgets, and dock pages, and the app has continued to receive updates through most of 2026, so it's actively maintained. It also includes a customizable news-reader widget and large-screen UI optimizations. Smart Launcher Pro — per its own pricing FAQ, a one-time purchase historically around the mid-$20s, though it's frequently discounted and worth checking current pricing — unlocks editable categories, multiple home-page widgets, popup widgets, and a blur effect, on top of the free tier's automatic sorting.

The free version already does the core job: install something, and it lands in a sensible category without you having to decide where it goes. That's a real convenience if your actual problem is "I have 80 apps and no idea where anything is," rather than "I have too many apps visible at once."

If your problem is genuinely about findability — too many apps, hard to locate the right one — Smart Launcher's automatic sorting solves that without asking you to give anything up. If your problem is about visual noise and temptation — every app sitting there is a potential distraction whether or not it's neatly filed — sorting it better doesn't remove the icons from view, and that's specifically the gap Thin Launcher's hard cap is built to close. A perfectly organized home screen full of categorized apps is still a home screen full of apps.

How to tell which fix actually matches your problem

A few honest questions worth asking yourself:

  • Do you forget where things are, or do you know exactly where they are and just wish they weren't all in view? The first is a findability problem — Smart Launcher's categories solve it directly. The second is an attention problem — a hard limit solves it more directly.
  • Do you actually use most of your installed apps regularly? If you genuinely rely on dozens of apps, organizing them well matters more than cutting them down, and Smart Launcher's automatic sorting keeps all of them reachable without extra setup.
  • Would you rather make a short, deliberate list once, or let an algorithm sort a long list for you continuously? That's really a question about how much decision-making you want upfront versus ongoing.

Both are legitimate answers to "my home screen is a mess" — they just start from different diagnoses of what's actually wrong.

What Thin Launcher does instead

Thin Launcher doesn't organize your full app list at all — it limits what's visible in the first place. Free users choose up to 7 apps during onboarding; Pro, a one-time purchase, raises that to 11 and adds colour themes. Everything else sits in the standard app drawer, reachable only by search, with no categories, folders, or auto-sorting layered on top. It's a deliberately smaller, less automated tool than Smart Launcher — the trade-off is fewer decisions made for you, in exchange for a shorter list you chose yourself.

Frequently asked questions

Is Smart Launcher still updated?

Yes — per its own changelog, Smart Launcher 6 received a version 6.6 update in January 2026 with a new dock system, and continued builds through much of 2026, indicating active, ongoing development.

Does Smart Launcher limit how many apps you can have?

No. Smart Launcher organizes every installed app into categories automatically rather than restricting how many are visible. Thin Launcher is the one that enforces a hard numeric cap — 7 apps free, 11 on Pro.

Which is better for someone who gets distracted by having too many apps visible?

If the issue is distraction from seeing too much at once, a hard-limit approach like Thin Launcher's addresses that more directly, since the apps themselves are out of view, not just better organized.
