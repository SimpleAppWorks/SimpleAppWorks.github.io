Action Launcher Alternative: Why Some Want a Simpler Home Screen Instead

Action Launcher is a deeply tweakable, actively maintained Pixel-style launcher for people who enjoy fine-tuning their phone. Thin Launcher is for people who'd rather stop tuning it. Here's the honest difference.

Simple App Works Published November 13, 2026 7 min read
QUICK ANSWER

Action Launcher (by developer Chris Lacy, branded "Action Launcher: Pixel Edition") is a free, actively maintained launcher built around Pixel-style aesthetics plus its own extras — Shutters, Covers, and quick search — with a one-time Plus purchase for the rest. It's built for people who like adjusting how their home screen looks and behaves. Thin Launcher is built for the opposite instinct: a hard cap of 7 apps free (11 on Pro, one-time purchase), with nothing else to tune. Neither replaces the other — they're different jobs.

Action Launcher has stayed in active development for years under a single developer, Chris Lacy, which is unusual in a category where several well-known names have gone quiet or changed hands recently. If you're searching for an "Action Launcher alternative," it's worth being honest about why you might be looking: either you want something with less to configure, or you want a different flavor of the same deep customization. Those lead to very different recommendations, and conflating them is a common way to end up disappointed with whatever you switch to next.

This post walks through what Action Launcher actually offers today, how that compares honestly to a hard-limit launcher like Thin Launcher, and a short set of questions to help you figure out which camp you're actually in before you install anything new.

What Action Launcher is actually built for

Per its own listing, Action Launcher: Pixel Edition is free to install, with recent versions (v50 and later) having opened up most premium features to free users — a notable, user-friendly change from its earlier paywall-heavier history. A Plus purchase, a one-time unlock rather than a subscription, covers what's left. Its signature features include Shutters (peek inside a folder icon without opening it), Covers (swap an app's icon for a live-ish preview), and Action Search, a unified search bar for apps, contacts, and more. It continues to receive updates — a release landed as recently as April 2026.

That's a specific kind of value: visual flourishes and shortcuts layered onto a Pixel-like base, for someone who wants their home screen to look considered and do a few clever tricks. It's squarely a power-user, customization-first launcher — not a minimalist one, and it doesn't market itself as one.

If part of the appeal of Action Launcher for you was genuinely the tweaking — picking which folders get Shutters, deciding which icons get Covers — that's a real preference, and a minimalist launcher will likely feel like it's taken something away rather than given you calm. If, on the other hand, you installed it for the Pixel look and never touched the extras, the thing you actually wanted might just be fewer icons, which a hard-limit launcher solves more directly than any amount of visual polish can.

How to tell which one you actually want

A few honest questions, since the right answer depends on you, not on either app's feature list:

  • Do you look forward to opening the settings menu? If arranging Shutters and Covers feels like a hobby rather than a chore, you're the audience Action Launcher is built for.
  • Did you install it mostly for the Pixel look, and never touch the extras? If so, the visual polish was doing the heavy lifting, not the deep customization — and a plain, calm home screen might get you the same feeling with less setup.
  • Is your actual complaint "too many icons pulling at my attention," not "I wish I had more tricks to arrange them with"? That's a different problem, and it's the one a hard app-count limit is built to solve.
  • Would losing Shutters or Covers feel like a real loss? If yes, stick with Action Launcher or something like it — switching to a minimalist launcher to fix an unrelated frustration usually just means reinstalling the old one a week later.

None of this is about which app is "better" in the abstract. Action Launcher is a mature, actively maintained tool for people who enjoy shaping their home screen's behaviour in detail. A hard-limit launcher is for people who'd rather not have that many decisions to make in the first place.

What Thin Launcher does instead

Thin Launcher skips the visual-trick layer entirely. Free users pick up to 7 apps for the home screen; Pro, a one-time purchase, raises that to 11 and adds more colour themes. Everything else lives in the standard app drawer, one search away. There's no Shutters-style folder peek, no icon-swap tricks, and no usage tracking — just a smaller, fixed set of apps in view and nothing else layered on top.

Frequently asked questions

Is Action Launcher still maintained?

Yes — per its release notes, Action Launcher: Pixel Edition has continued receiving updates from developer Chris Lacy, with a release as recently as April 2026.

Is Action Launcher free?

It's free to install, with most features now available without payment following recent updates. A one-time Plus purchase unlocks what remains, including unlimited search engine choice in supported countries.

Does Action Launcher have a hard app limit like Thin Launcher?

No. Action Launcher doesn't restrict how many apps you can place on your home screen — its minimalism, such as it is, comes from a clean Pixel-style look, not a numeric cap.
