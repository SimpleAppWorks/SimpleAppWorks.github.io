Nova Launcher Alternative: Why Some Switch to a Simpler Launcher

Nova Launcher and Thin Launcher aren't really competing for the same job. One is built to give you more control. The other is built to give you less to control. Here's how to tell which one you actually want.

Simple App Works Published November 13, 2026 8 min read
QUICK ANSWER

Nova Launcher is a deep-customization launcher — gestures, icon packs, scroll effects, granular "look and feel" controls — aimed at power users who want to configure everything. Thin Launcher is the opposite instinct: a hard limit of 7 apps free (11 on Pro, one-time purchase) and nothing to configure beyond that. They're not really alternatives to each other in the usual sense; they solve opposite problems. If Nova's recent ownership changes have you looking around anyway, this is what each one is actually for.

"Nova Launcher alternative" is a strange search to end up on a minimalist-launcher blog for, so it's worth being upfront: this isn't a "we're better than Nova" post. Nova Launcher is, by most honest accounts, one of the most capable Android launchers ever built. If what you loved about it was the depth — the gestures, the icon customization, the scroll effects — nothing here replaces that, and we're not going to pretend otherwise.

What this post is for: if you're searching around because Nova's recent news has made you nervous about relying on it, or because you've realized you never actually use most of what Nova offers, it's worth understanding what kind of launcher you're actually looking for before you pick the next one.

What's actually going on with Nova Launcher

This part is worth getting right, because there's been real uncertainty. In September 2025, TeslaCoil Software announced that development on Nova Launcher was stopping — its founder and, by then, sole developer had left the company that owned it, and the announcement said there would be no further updates, bug fixes, or new features. That understandably alarmed a lot of long-time users; a petition asking for an open-source release picked up well over a thousand signatures.

Then, in January 2026, Nova Launcher announced it had been acquired by Instabridge, a Swedish company, with a public statement that Nova was "here to stay" and a commitment to keep it stable, compatible with modern Android, and actively maintained going forward. So as of this writing, Nova is not abandoned — it has new ownership and a stated intent to keep developing it. Some users have also reported ad-related code (Facebook Ads SDK, AdMob) appearing in recent builds under the new owner, which is a separate point some long-time users are watching closely.

We're not going to speculate further than that. If you want the latest on Nova's maintenance status, its own Play Store listing and release notes are the most current source — things move fast with an app mid-transition between owners.

What Nova Launcher is actually built for

Nova Launcher's whole identity is customization depth. Per its own listing and long-standing feature set, the free version already gives you a generous "Look and Feel" menu — icon packs, icon labels, app drawer layout, hiding the notification bar, and more. Nova Prime, a one-time unlock (historically priced a few dollars, with Nova running frequent sales down to $0.99 or less over the years), adds a layer most casual users never touch: ten-plus home screen gestures (swipe, pinch, double-tap), custom notification badges, drawer folders, and more than ten scroll transition effects.

That's a lot of dials. For the person who wants to spend time tuning exactly how their home screen looks and behaves, Nova gives them more knobs to turn than almost anything else on the Play Store. That's a real, valid thing to want — it's just a completely different instinct from what a minimalist launcher is for.

Neither side of that table is the "upgrade" from the other. Nova is for people who find a highly tunable home screen satisfying — more control feels like more comfort. Thin Launcher is for the opposite instinct: people for whom more options feel like more friction, and who'd rather have fewer decisions to make every time they unlock their phone. Thin Launcher's entire mechanic is a hard cap — 7 apps on the free tier, 11 if you unlock Pro with a one-time purchase — with everything else living one search away in the drawer. There's no gesture library, no scroll-effect picker, no icon-size slider. That's not a missing feature; it's the point.

How to tell which one you actually want

A few honest questions, since the real answer depends on you, not on either app's marketing:

  • Did you ever open Nova's settings for fun? If tweaking gestures and scroll effects was part of the appeal, not a chore, you're the audience Nova is built for — a simpler launcher will likely feel like a downgrade, not a relief.
  • Did most of Nova's settings menu go untouched? If you installed it once, picked an icon pack, and never opened "Look and Feel" again, the depth wasn't doing much for you day to day.
  • Is your actual complaint "too many apps competing for my attention," not "not enough customization"? That's a different problem than anything Nova's feature set addresses, and it's the specific problem a hard app-count limit is built to solve.
  • Are you here because of the ownership uncertainty, not the feature set? That's a legitimate reason to look around — but it's worth picking the next launcher based on what you actually want it to do, not just because the old one made headlines.

If you read that list and recognized yourself in the second half, a hard-limit launcher like Thin Launcher is worth trying. If you recognized yourself in the first half, Nova (under its new ownership) or another deep-customization launcher is still the more honest fit — switching away from something you genuinely enjoyed configuring, just because of unrelated news, usually ends in reinstalling it a week later.

What Thin Launcher does instead

Thin Launcher doesn't try to be a lighter version of Nova. It starts from a different premise: your home screen has too many places for your attention to land, and the fix isn't better organization — it's fewer things to organize. Free users choose up to 7 apps for their home screen; everything else lives in the drawer, one search away. Pro (a one-time purchase, not a subscription) raises that to 11 and adds more colour themes. There's no gesture system, no scroll-effect library, and no usage tracking or app-blocking — it changes what's visible, not what's installed, and it doesn't watch what you do with either.

Frequently asked questions

Is Nova Launcher discontinued?

It went through a scare: TeslaCoil Software announced in September 2025 that development had stopped after its founder departed. In January 2026, Instabridge announced it had acquired Nova Launcher and stated it would keep the app maintained going forward. As of this writing, Nova is not abandoned, but check its current Play Store listing for the latest status.

Is Thin Launcher a replacement for Nova Launcher's features?

No, and it isn't trying to be. Nova is built around deep customization — gestures, scroll effects, icon tuning. Thin Launcher is built around a hard app-count limit and almost nothing else to configure. They solve different problems for different people.

What's a good alternative if I liked Nova's customization?

If deep customization is what you valued, Nova under its new ownership, or another power-user launcher, is a more honest fit than a minimalist launcher. A hard-limit launcher like Thin Launcher is for the opposite instinct — wanting fewer choices, not more ways to configure them.
