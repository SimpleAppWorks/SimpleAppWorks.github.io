Niagara Launcher vs Thin Launcher: Two Takes on a Minimalist Home Screen

Niagara Launcher and Thin Launcher are both genuinely minimalist, but they get there differently — a curated list versus a hard cap. Here's how the two approaches actually differ.

Simple App Works Published November 13, 2026 8 min read
QUICK ANSWER

Niagara Launcher and Thin Launcher are both built around a short, deliberate home screen, but the mechanism differs. Niagara shows a scrollable favorites list plus an alphabetical "wave" shortcut to jump to any installed app, with a top widget slot — per its own listing, its free tier is ad-free and its Pro upgrade (reported around $13.99/year or roughly $42.99 lifetime) adds further customization. Thin Launcher instead enforces a hard numeric cap — 7 apps free, 11 on Pro (a one-time purchase, not a subscription) — with everything else only reachable through search. Niagara gives you a longer curated list with quick access to everything; Thin Launcher gives you a shorter, fixed list and nothing else in view.

Unlike Nova Launcher, Niagara is actually a close relative to Thin Launcher in spirit — both exist specifically to cut down a busy home screen rather than to maximize how much you can configure. That makes this comparison more apples-to-apples than most "vs" posts in this space, but the two apps still solve the problem with meaningfully different mechanics. Here's what's actually different, based on each app's own public listing and documentation.

How Niagara Launcher's home screen actually works

Niagara's home screen is a scrollable list, not a grid: a short set of favorite apps sits front and center, with one widget slot above it (commonly used for the clock, weather, or media controls). To open anything else, you use what Niagara calls "wave" navigation — an alphabet strip along the right edge of the screen that jumps you straight to any installed app by its first letter, without opening a separate app drawer screen. Sliding down brings up full search across apps and contacts. The list is also adaptive, meaning it can surface things like an active media player or an upcoming calendar event contextually, not just a static set of icons.

Per its listing, Niagara's free tier is genuinely free to use and ad-free, with the core list-and-wave experience, icon pack support, and basic pop-up folders included. Niagara Pro — reported at roughly $13.99/year or a lifetime option around $42.99, per pricing trackers and its own help documentation, though you should check its current listing since launcher pricing changes — unlocks deeper theming and customization on top of that free core.

How Thin Launcher's home screen actually works

Thin Launcher takes a blunter approach: instead of a longer curated list with quick access to everything else, it enforces a hard numeric limit on how many apps can sit on the home screen at all. Free users choose up to 7 apps during onboarding; Pro, a one-time purchase rather than a subscription, raises that ceiling to 11 and unlocks more colour themes. There's no alphabet-wave shortcut and no adaptive widget slot — anything outside your chosen set lives in the standard app drawer, reached by search, with deliberately nothing extra layered on top of that search step.

Thin Launcher also doesn't track screen time or block apps by schedule, the same as Niagara doesn't — both are layout tools, not usage-monitoring tools. If what you're after is a timer or a blocking schedule, neither app is built for that; see our note on that distinction in our broader minimalist launcher comparison.

The most practical difference is this: Niagara's list can hold more apps than Thin Launcher's cap ever allows, because Niagara's minimalism comes from presentation (a clean list, contextual info, quick alphabet access) rather than a hard ceiling. Thin Launcher's minimalism comes specifically from the ceiling itself — once you've picked your 7 (or 11), there is no "quick access to everything else" layered on top; you search, the same as you would in stock Android's app drawer, and that extra half-second of friction is intentional rather than something to optimize away.

On pricing: subscription vs one-time

One difference worth calling out plainly, since it matters to a lot of people choosing between minimalist launchers: per the pricing information publicly available for Niagara Pro, it offers both a recurring annual subscription and a lifetime option. Thin Launcher has never offered a subscription at all — Ad-Free and Pro are each a single one-time purchase, alongside optional one-time tips. If avoiding subscriptions entirely is part of why you're looking at minimalist launchers in the first place, that's a concrete difference, not just a marketing line.

Which approach actually fits you

A rough way to decide:

  • You like having a slightly longer list with fast alphabet access, plus a widget up top? Niagara's list-based approach is built exactly for that, and its free tier is genuinely usable without paying.
  • You want to force yourself into a strict number and stop there, full stop? Thin Launcher's hard cap is the more deliberate constraint — there's no "just add one more to the list" because the list itself has a ceiling.
  • Subscriptions are a dealbreaker for you? Thin Launcher's one-time-purchase-only model is worth knowing about upfront; check Niagara's current listing if a subscription would change your decision.
  • You want colour themes without extra configuration? Thin Launcher's theme set is unlocked via Pro or a rewarded ad, with no further settings beyond picking one.

Both apps are aimed at the same underlying frustration — too many icons competing for a glance you only meant to spend three seconds on — and neither is the objectively "correct" answer. Frequently asked questions

Is Niagara Launcher free?

Yes — per its own listing, Niagara's free tier is ad-free and includes its core favorites-list-and-wave-navigation experience. Niagara Pro is a separate paid upgrade for deeper customization.

Does Niagara Launcher have a hard app limit like Thin Launcher?

No. Niagara's minimalism comes from its list-based layout and alphabet navigation, not a fixed numeric cap. Thin Launcher specifically enforces a hard limit — 7 apps free, 11 on Pro.

Which one has no subscription?

Thin Launcher has no subscription option at all — Ad-Free and Pro are one-time purchases. Niagara Pro, per its pricing information, offers both an annual subscription and a lifetime purchase option.

Prefer a fixed limit over a longer list?

Free to start, one-time purchase to unlock Pro.
