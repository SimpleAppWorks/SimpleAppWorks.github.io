Evie Launcher Alternative: What to Try Now That It's Off the Play Store

Evie Launcher was once one of the most popular "dead-simple" launchers on Android. It was pulled from the Play Store in the early 2020s. Here's an honest look at what its old fans actually want now.

Simple App Works Published November 13, 2026 7 min read
QUICK ANSWER

Evie Launcher, once marketed as a "dead-simple" free launcher built around fast universal search, was removed from the Play Store in the early 2020s and is no longer available to download there. If you're searching for an Evie Launcher alternative, the honest answer depends on what you actually miss: its instant search, or its overall simplicity. A minimalist, hard-limit launcher like Thin Launcher recreates the second (fewer apps, calmer screen) but is not a drop-in replacement for the first — search-first launchers and limit-first launchers solve the clutter problem differently.

If you landed here searching for "Evie Launcher alternative," there's a decent chance it's because the app itself is gone, not because you were comparing features. That's worth addressing directly and honestly before recommending anything.

This post covers what's actually known about Evie's disappearance, what its fans specifically valued about it, and two honestly different directions depending on which part of that experience you're trying to recreate — rather than assuming every ex-Evie user wants the same replacement.

What actually happened to Evie Launcher

Evie Launcher launched years ago with a clear pitch: a free, fast, "dead-simple" home screen built around instant search across apps and contacts, without the clutter most launchers added on top. It built a loyal following for exactly that simplicity. At some point in the early 2020s, Evie Launcher was removed from the Google Play Store, and it has not reappeared there as of this writing. Public reporting on exactly why is thin and somewhat inconsistent, so rather than guess at a reason, the honest summary is: it's gone, and if you're looking for it now, you won't find it on the Play Store.

That leaves its old users in a specific spot — not deciding between Evie and something else, but needing a new launcher entirely because the old one simply isn't there anymore.

What people actually missed about Evie

Evie's appeal split roughly two ways, and it matters which one was yours:

  • The search. Evie's instant, unified search across apps and contacts was fast and central to the experience — you typed, and the right thing appeared, rather than hunting through icons.
  • The simplicity. It was free, clean, and didn't ask you to configure much of anything — a calm, no-fuss home screen by default.

If it was mainly the search you miss, a minimalist launcher built around a hard app-count limit isn't really filling the same role — it's solving a different problem (fewer apps visible) rather than making search itself faster or smarter. If it was the overall simplicity and lack of clutter, a hard-limit launcher is a much closer match, arguably an even more deliberate version of what Evie was going for.

Other directions worth knowing about

Since Evie itself isn't an option anymore, it's worth naming the two broad directions clearly rather than assuming one fits everyone who remembers it fondly. If search speed was the draw, look at launchers that market a unified search bar as their main feature — that's a different category from this post's focus and deserves its own research rather than a guess here. If the draw was "it didn't ask much of me," minimalist, low-configuration launchers are the closer modern equivalent, and a hard app-count limit is one specific, honest way that category solves the clutter problem.

Either way, it's worth resisting the urge to pick whatever's most hyped right now just because the thing you actually used is gone. The two problems — slow search and a cluttered screen — have different fixes, and conflating them is how people end up reinstalling three launchers in a month. Taking a moment to name which part of Evie you actually miss is a better use of five minutes than installing the first "Evie Launcher alternative" result and hoping it fits.

What Thin Launcher offers instead

Thin Launcher doesn't try to reproduce Evie's specific search experience — it takes a blunter route to the same calm outcome. Free users choose up to 7 apps for their home screen; Pro, a one-time purchase (not a subscription), raises that to 11 and adds colour themes. Everything else lives in the standard Android app drawer, reachable through the drawer's own search, exactly as it would on stock Android — Thin Launcher doesn't add a custom search layer on top. What it does add is the thing Evie never had: a hard, enforced limit on how many apps can sit on the home screen at all, so the "clutter" problem is addressed before you'd ever need to search your way out of it.

If instant, unified search across your whole phone was really what you valued, it's worth looking specifically for a search-first launcher rather than a limit-first one — they're not interchangeable, and we'd rather say that plainly than pretend otherwise. Frequently asked questions

Is Evie Launcher still available?

No — it was removed from the Google Play Store in the early 2020s and has not returned there as of this writing.

Is Thin Launcher a replacement for Evie's search feature?

Not specifically. Thin Launcher relies on the standard Android app drawer and its built-in search rather than adding its own unified search layer. Its distinct feature is a hard limit on home screen apps, which Evie never had.

What should I look for if I mainly miss Evie's fast search?

Look specifically for a search-first launcher, since that's a different design goal than a hard-limit minimalist launcher like Thin Launcher. If what you actually miss is the lack of clutter, a hard-limit launcher is the closer match.
