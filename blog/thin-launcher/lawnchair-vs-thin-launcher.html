Lawnchair vs Thin Launcher: Free Customization vs a Hard Limit

Lawnchair is free, open-source, and built to let you customize everything the Pixel Launcher locks down. Thin Launcher takes almost everything off the table on purpose. Here's the honest difference.

Simple App Works Published November 13, 2026 7 min read
QUICK ANSWER

Lawnchair is a free, open-source launcher built on the same base as the Google Pixel Launcher, and per its own description it lets you customize grid size, icon packs, dock layout, and gestures that the stock Pixel Launcher locks down — it can even import your old Nova Launcher backup. It's entirely free, with no ads and no subscription. Thin Launcher takes the opposite approach: a hard cap of 7 apps free (11 on Pro, one-time purchase), with no grid tuning, no gesture system, and nothing to import. Lawnchair gives you more knobs for free; Thin Launcher removes most of the knobs on purpose.

Lawnchair occupies an interesting spot in this space: it's a legitimate, free, actively developed alternative to paid customization launchers like Nova — close enough in spirit that it can directly import a Nova Launcher backup, per its own documentation. If you came from Nova and don't want to pay for Prime-style features elsewhere, Lawnchair is a genuinely relevant option to know about, even though it's not what this post is ultimately recommending for a minimalist use case.

That makes this a slightly unusual match-up: a free, open-source, deeply configurable launcher against a paid-but-minimal one with almost no configuration at all. Worth laying out plainly rather than glossing over, since "free" and "simple" aren't the same thing, even though people sometimes search as if they are.

What Lawnchair is actually built for

Lawnchair is open source, based on Launcher3 from the Android Open Source Project — the same codebase underlying Google's own Pixel Launcher. Because the code is public, per its own materials, Lawnchair tends to pick up new Pixel Launcher features quickly, while adding customization the stock version doesn't allow: grid size, icon pack support, dock configuration, and gesture mapping. It's entirely free, with no subscription plan to unlock features or remove ads — there's nothing to unlock, because there's no paywall at all. The project has continued active development into 2026, with a recent beta release in April 2026.

This makes Lawnchair a strong fit for someone who wants a Pixel-like, highly tunable experience without paying for Nova Prime or Action Launcher's Plus tier. It is not, however, trying to be minimalist — more customization, not fewer apps, is the whole point.

If what drew you to Lawnchair (or away from a paid customization launcher) was specifically the price — wanting deep control without a purchase — Lawnchair solves exactly that, and switching to a minimalist launcher instead would mean giving up the control you were specifically looking for. If what you actually want is to stop tuning a grid at all and just see fewer things when you unlock your phone, that's a different goal than anything Lawnchair's feature set is built around, no matter how good or free it is. Being free doesn't make a launcher minimalist — it just makes deep customization cheaper to reach, which is a genuinely different kind of value than having fewer decisions to make in the first place.

How to tell which one you actually want

A few honest questions:

  • Do you enjoy picking a grid size, an icon pack, and a gesture set, or does that sound like homework? If it sounds enjoyable, Lawnchair gives you that for free, with nothing locked behind a paywall.
  • Is price the only reason you're considering a minimalist launcher? If you'd happily keep a fully customizable launcher as long as it didn't cost anything, Lawnchair already solves that — switching to a hard-limit launcher wouldn't be solving the problem you actually have.
  • Do you want to stop making layout decisions entirely, not just stop paying for the ability to make them? That's the specific problem a hard app-count limit addresses, and no amount of free, open-source tooling changes that goal.

Lawnchair and Thin Launcher aren't competing for the same user — one removes the price tag from deep customization, the other removes the customization itself.

What Thin Launcher does instead

Thin Launcher doesn't offer a grid to configure, because it isn't trying to replace your home screen layout — it's trying to shrink it. Free users pick up to 7 apps; Pro, a one-time purchase (not a subscription), raises that to 11 and adds more colour themes. There's no icon pack system, no gesture mapping, and no import tool, because there's very little to import or configure in the first place. Everything outside your chosen set lives in the standard app drawer, one search away.

Frequently asked questions

Is Lawnchair really free?

Yes — per its own materials, Lawnchair is free and open source with no ads and no subscription or in-app purchases to unlock features.

Can I import my Nova Launcher setup into Lawnchair?

Per Lawnchair's own documentation, it supports importing a Nova Launcher backup, including home screen and dock grid layout, widgets, folders, and a selected icon pack.

Does Lawnchair limit how many apps you can have on your home screen?

No — Lawnchair lets you customize your grid size yourself, with no enforced cap. Thin Launcher is the one that enforces a hard numeric limit: 7 apps free, 11 on Pro.
